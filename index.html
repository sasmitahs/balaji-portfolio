I’m a mathematics PhD student at Carnegie Mellon University, where I focus on problems in mathematical finance. I’m interested in stochastic analysis and partial differential equations, and their connections to learning and decision-making.

My background includes a BTech in Mathematics and Computer Science at IISc, research at Purdue on physics-informed methods for diffusion hitting-time PDEs and nonparametric impulse control, and quantitative risk work at HSBC on FRTB stress scenarios. My broader interests span generative diffusion models, high-dimensional sampling, reinforcement learning, and stochastic games.

A Network Formation Game for Katz Centrality Maximization: A Resource Allocation Perspective

Balaji R, Prashil Wankhede, Pavankumar Tallapragada

65th IEEE Conference on Decision and Control · Honolulu, Hawai‘i

How do networks take shape when each agent has limited resources and wants to maximize its influence? We study this question through Katz centrality, strategic resource allocation, and the networks that emerge at equilibrium.

Research overview

The work characterizes Nash equilibrium networks and studies a sequential best-response process that converges under mild assumptions. For complete underlying networks, equilibrium centralities are proportional to agents’ budgets. With general topologies and self-loops, the equilibria exhibit a hierarchical structure.
